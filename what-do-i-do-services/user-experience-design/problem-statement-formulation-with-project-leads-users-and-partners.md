# Problem Statement Formulation With Project Leads, Users and Partners

## Purpose

The purpose of problem definition:

* Defines user specific goals.
* It is a way of building a concise description of what we are attempting to solve.&#x20;
* It helps us create a working to-do list that specifically prioritises the challenges that our users face by defining our deliverables relative to their needs.
* Creates benchmarks for success

## Methodology

This is sometimes done live in a needs assessments or follow up sessions with partners and clients depending on the context and how much we already know about our user base.

The process generally rolls out as follows

* The salient points from the [**needs assessments**](#user-content-fn-1)[^1] are collected in their relevant themes as part of the [**brief development**](#user-content-fn-2)[^2] phase.
* These points are reframed in a clear sentence that allows us to humanise the participants further and understand some core points about them. This reframing can be done like this:
  * **\[Name of user persona]** is a **\[type of user]** who needs **\[type of user experience]** because **\[benefits of user experience]**.
  * As an example: "**Berta** is an **older person with a visual impairment**. She is a long-time bakery customer who prefers ordering over the phone. **She needs a website and online ordering system that are easy to use, adapt to her vision needs, and mimic the feel and flow of a friendly phone conversation**. **She wants to place her orders with ease** and feel like she’s making a personal connection with the bakery.
* Connect the pain points to the problem statements by understanding the 5 W's + 1H:
  * **Who** is experiencing the problem? Knowing your users and their background is key to creating successful solutions for them.
  * **What** are the pain points you’re trying to solve? Determining a user’s pain points early allows you to answer the rest of these questions and clarify the context of the pain points.
  * **Where** is the user when they’re using the product? A user’s physical context matters to your design.
  * **When** does the problem occur? Maybe it’s right after the end of a long and tedious process, or maybe it’s something that happens daily. Knowing when the problem occurs can help you better empathize with the user’s feelings.&#x20;
  * **Why** is the problem important? Knowing how this problem affects your user’s experience and life will help to clarify the potential consequences.
  * **How** are users reaching their goals by using the product? Understanding how users reach their goals allows you to map the user journey that they take through your product.
* The challenges are collected and prioritised according to:
  * How critical they are for success (how many users need this challenge to be addressed?)
  * How difficult/time intensive they are to execute&#x20;
  * _This is sometimes done using a_ [_<mark style="color:blue;">prioritisation matrix</mark>_](https://www.figma.com/design/dCZlmQzdsxVCE0HqixxuVi/Design-Domain-Playbook-References?node-id=6-1456\&t=vAJaGY5EF2F08u6t-1) _like this one_
* The value of each challenge to be addressed is then judged on it's merit and these features are further broken down into groups like:
  * Do Now - Critical challenges that need to be solved at the start
  * Do Later - Future features backlog items that need time or budget or research first
  * Don't Do These - Aspects that our users have confirmed that are not useful to them
